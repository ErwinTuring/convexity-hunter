# ADR-007: Strike and Delta generation is mode-based, not universal

Status: Accepted
Decision date: 2026-07-30

## Context

A universal 5–10 Delta generation policy expresses one extreme-tail approach,
but it does not fit every Convexity Hunter hypothesis. Systemic or extreme
one-sided tails, ordinary event-directional tails, bidirectional distribution
expansion, and direct user-supplied structures require different geometry.

Low dollar premium is not proof of relatively cheap convexity. Deep
out-of-the-money options may have expensive tail implied volatility, wide
spreads, weak liquidity, or model-sensitive values. They do not necessarily
have high initial Gamma or maximum Vega. A 25 Delta structure remains useful
for event research, tail-pricing comparison, and wing-curvature evidence.

## Decision

Discovery generation classifies the declared distribution-change hypothesis
into one of three modes:

1. **Extreme-tail mode.** For a specific one-sided extreme downside or upside
   hypothesis, signed option Delta is retained as evidence and absolute Delta
   determines range qualification. The initial primary tier is
   `0.05 <= abs(delta) <= 0.10`. The fallback tier is
   `0.10 < abs(delta) <= 0.15` and is used only when the primary tier cannot
   provide an eligible real listed contract with sufficient required market
   evidence. The exploratory far-tail tier is
   `0.02 <= abs(delta) < 0.05`; it is not generated by default and requires a
   real listed contract, valid two-sided quote, usable liquidity evidence,
   available IV and reference evidence, calculable total entry cost, and
   responsible scenario valuation. Structures above 0.15 absolute Delta are
   outside the default extreme-tail grammar, not globally prohibited.
2. **Event-directional convexity mode.** For a meaningful directional event
   tail that need not be a systemic or extreme-market crash, representative
   targets remain approximately 10 Delta and approximately 25 Delta. The
   farther 10 Delta region generally has lower absolute premium and may offer
   a higher value multiple but requires a larger move. The less remote 25
   Delta region usually costs more and may respond earlier to a material event
   move. Neither is automatically preferred or ranked. A 25 Delta structure
   remains a possible event-directional candidate, tail-pricing comparison
   point, and wing-curvature evidence anchor. A future deterministic contract
   must define the Delta convention, nearest-eligible-Delta resolution, tie
   handling, expiration interaction, and quote and liquidity qualification.
3. **Bidirectional distribution-expansion mode.** When direction is unresolved
   but the future return distribution may widen, the active MVP uses an ATM or
   near-ATM Long Straddle because Long Strangle is unsupported. A future
   deterministic contract must disclose the exact ATM reference, without this
   ADR choosing spot, forward, or another method.

These modes govern discovery generation. A supported structure supplied
through direct user entry is not rejected solely because it falls outside the
default discovery ranges. It must still pass real-contract verification, the
supported Long Option Position grammar, DTE policy, quote and reference
validation, market-data provenance, calculation lineage, liquidity and cost
analysis, and Convexity Engine research. Reports may disclose that it does not
correspond to a default discovery mode.

Delta is not fixed percentage moneyness. Its disclosed inputs and methodology
include underlying price, strike, remaining maturity, implied volatility,
rates, dividends, surface shape, and pricing convention. Generated structures
must separately disclose actual strike distance.

Mode-specific structures require mode-appropriate scenarios. The implemented
general v0.1 grid remains the baseline. Future extreme-tail coverage must add
directionally aligned event-level, severe, and extreme-tail stress, with
versioned calibration that may differ by asset and event type. IV shocks must
show base and shocked IV and whether the change uses relative percentages or
absolute volatility points. A large value multiple is evidence, not a sole
rule for `Investigate`.

The user selects the final exact structure, and the system does not rank
structures by attractiveness.

## Rationale

This policy preserves the asymmetry sought in extreme-tail research without
forcing every event, Long Call, Long Put, or Long Straddle into the same
geometry. It also keeps generation distinct from the Convexity Engine's job:
testing volatility environment, tail-relative pricing, liquidity, total cost,
Theta, Gamma-cost relationship, expiration thresholds, non-expiration
scenarios, and risk assumptions to determine whether generated convexity may
actually be cheap.

## Rejected alternatives

- All candidates must be 5–10 Delta.
- All structures above 20 Delta are globally rejected.
- 25 Delta is never researchable.
- ATM structures are globally prohibited.
- One high-multiple scenario automatically produces `Investigate`.

## Consequences

- Future structure generation must classify the hypothesis mode.
- Future Delta and ATM resolution must be deterministic and disclosed.
- Future scenarios must be mode-appropriate.
- The Convexity Engine still tests whether generated convexity is actually
  cheap.
- No universal annual tail-protection budget is imposed. Any future annual
  convexity-budget value must be supplied by the caller or user and remains a
  future risk-contract question, distinct from single-structure maximum loss
  and repeated-failure cost.
- Vanna and Volga do not become required MVP fields.
